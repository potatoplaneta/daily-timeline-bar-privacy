DAILY TIMELINE BAR FOR MAC

Privacy Policy

How Daily Timeline Bar reads calendars, stores preferences, handles purchases, and keeps you in control of your information.

Effective and last updated: October 21, 2026

At a glance

  • Your calendar events and settings are processed on your Mac.
  • Calendar access is read-only. The app never creates, edits, or deletes events.
  • No developer-operated backend, advertising, or third-party analytics SDKs.
  • Apple handles the free trial, purchases, and restoration through the App Store.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy applies to the Daily Timeline Bar macOS app and its privacy and support website. The app is maintained by PotatoPlanetStudios. You do not need a separate Daily Timeline Bar account.

The app does not send your calendar events, schedules, or preferences to the developer. We do not sell personal data, use advertising trackers, or include third-party analytics SDKs.

2. Calendar access and event information

With your permission, Daily Timeline Bar uses Apple’s EventKit framework to read calendars available on your Mac. These can include iCloud, Google, Microsoft Exchange, CalDAV, and subscribed calendars that you have configured in Apple Calendar or macOS Internet Accounts. macOS and your calendar provider manage sign-in and synchronization. Daily Timeline Bar does not sign in to those providers directly or receive their passwords.

The app reads calendar names, source names, identifiers, and colors so you can choose which calendars to show. For selected calendars, it reads event titles, start and end times, all-day and busy status, cancellation status, your attendance response, location text, event identifiers, and supported event or meeting URLs. It uses this information for timeline blocks, labels, your agenda, and optional reminders.

The app keeps a local snapshot of nearby events to display them between refreshes. It does not save event notes, attachments, or a list of attendees. Location text is an event’s saved location, not your device’s GPS location.

Opening an event link or choosing to join a meeting opens the relevant website or application. That destination handles the request under its own privacy practices. The app does not visit meeting links automatically.

3. Information stored and used on your Mac

Daily Timeline Bar stores your weekly hours, breaks, date exceptions, time zone, themes, label preferences, keyboard shortcuts, reminder preferences, display selections, onboarding state, and calendar selections locally. Saved display selections can contain display identifiers. A recovery copy of the previous settings file may also be kept.

Settings and calendar snapshots are stored inside the app’s sandbox, in its Application Support/Daily Timeline Bar folder. The app does not provide its own cloud synchronization. Settings exports are saved only to a file you choose; they do not include cached calendar events, account credentials, or purchase entitlements. Your exports can still contain personal schedule and display preferences, so review them before sharing.

The app uses display geometry, menu-bar appearance, pointer position, and available window bounds and stacking information locally to place the timeline, show hover labels, and keep it below menus. It does not record your screen, capture keystrokes, or upload application activity.

Optional reminders are local macOS notifications. Their content can include event titles, subject to the app’s privacy settings. macOS controls notification display, history, and retention. The app does not operate a push-notification service.

The troubleshooting tool checks relevant preferences and system state locally. Its diagnostic summary includes app and macOS versions, permission status, calendar and display counts, settings, checks, and actions taken. It excludes event titles, calendar names, email addresses, display identifiers, and credentials. Nothing is sent automatically: you can preview the summary, copy it, and decide whether to include it in a support email.

4. Free trial and App Store purchases

Apple processes the manually started 7-day trial, the one-time Full Access purchase, restoration, and refunds through StoreKit and the App Store. The trial does not renew or charge you automatically. Daily Timeline Bar does not receive your payment card details or Apple Account password.

The app uses Apple-verified transaction identifiers, product identifiers, original purchase dates, and revocation information to determine access. StoreKit supplies the transaction history, including available verified history for offline use.

A separate, device-only Keychain record stores the latest observed date to reduce accidental trial extension after a clock change. This timestamp does not contain calendar data or payment details, and the app does not request its synchronization through iCloud Keychain. Removing local app files does not erase Apple’s transaction history or restart a previously activated trial.

These records are not uploaded to the developer. Apple handles its account and transaction data under Apple’s privacy policy. Apple may make App Store sales reports, analytics, or diagnostics available to developers through its own services and your Apple privacy settings. Daily Timeline Bar does not send custom analytics events to us.

5. Permissions and optional features

  • Calendars: macOS calls this permission Full Access. Daily Timeline Bar uses it only to read events. You can use the schedule and progress timeline without connecting a calendar.
  • Notifications: requested when you choose to enable reminders. You can turn them off in the app or in macOS notification settings.
  • Files you select: used to import or export preferences through macOS file pickers.
  • Start with your Mac: if enabled, macOS registers the app to launch at login.

Calendar access can be revoked in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars. Notification and login-item controls are also available in System Settings. The timeline does not require Screen Recording or Accessibility control permission.

6. Retention, deletion, and your choices

Preferences remain on your Mac until changed or removed. Calendar snapshots are replaced during refreshes and may remain while the app is closed or unable to read calendars. Changing a display filter or hiding labels does not erase cached event details.

  • Clear saved events: choose Settings → Privacy → Clear cache. This clears the app’s event snapshot, but a later refresh can read events again while Apple Calendar remains connected.
  • Stop calendar reads and remove the connection: choose Settings → Calendars → Disconnect. This removes the app’s cached connection and events, including any calendar-cache backup. It does not remove your accounts or events from Apple Calendar. Revoke the macOS calendar permission as well if you want to prevent further access.
  • Remove preferences and their recovery copy: quit the app, then remove its Daily Timeline Bar Application Support folder. For sandboxed builds, the usual path is ~/Library/Containers/com.PotatoPlanetStudios.DailyTimelineBar/Data/Library/Application Support/Daily Timeline Bar/. Remove any settings exports you saved separately if you no longer need them.
  • Remove residual local records: macOS preferences and the Keychain item with service com.PotatoPlanetStudios.DailyTimelineBar.purchaseClock can remain after app removal. Contact support for help with a complete local reset. Apple’s purchase history remains governed by Apple.
  • Control visible information: use Privacy settings to redact private events or chosen calendars, or enable presentation mode. Manage already-delivered notifications in macOS Notification Center.

Uninstalling the app alone may leave local support files, preferences, or Keychain records. The developer cannot access or remotely delete information stored only on your Mac. Backups you make may contain copies of local files; manage those copies through your backup provider.

If you email potatoplanet.studios@gmail.com, we receive your email address, message, and any attachments you choose to send. We use them to respond and troubleshoot your request. Correspondence may remain in the support mailbox until deleted; you can request deletion of messages we control. Our Gmail mailbox is provided by Google, whose processing is described in the Google Privacy Policy. Email services may retain copies under their own policies.

GitHub Issues are public and require a GitHub account. Your username and issue content are visible to others and remain on GitHub until removed, subject to GitHub’s retention policies. You can edit or delete content where GitHub permits, or ask us for help with content we control.

Do not post private calendar information, event titles, meeting links, passwords, or purchase records in public issues. Use email for private requests, and share only what is needed to answer your question.
